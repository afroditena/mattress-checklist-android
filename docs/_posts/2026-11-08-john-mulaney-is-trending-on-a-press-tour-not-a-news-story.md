A comedian explained how he uses a pair of sweatpants as a makeshift harness while his kid learns to pedal, and that was enough to push his name into US Google Search trends on the evening of Saturday, November 7, 2026. No scandal, no announcement, no casting bombshell. Just a red-carpet interview clip doing what red-carpet interview clips do.

## What's Happening

Two separate Mulaney items landed within about 48 hours of each other, and both came off the same event.

According to E! News, Mulaney described at the Los Angeles premiere of the film *Madden* how he's currently teaching his and Olivia Munn's son to ride a bike — looping sweatpants under the kid's arms and holding the legs from behind, a trick he was careful to say he didn't invent. He told E! News' Will Marfuggi that he can ride a bike himself but has no idea how to explain it. Worth flagging: E!'s own copy and a Yahoo syndication of it give different dates for the premiere, November 4 versus November 5, so I'd treat the exact day as unsettled.

The second item is the more substantive one. Mulaney told The Hollywood Reporter on the carpet that the *Get Smart* reboot is "a world of espionage with a super, super confident, not always informed person," which he called his favorite type of character to play. THR reports Seth Grahame-Smith is writing the screenplay, with Charles Roven and Andrew Lazar producing.

The movie that put him on that carpet is *Madden*. Amazon's own announcement says the film premieres November 18 on Prime Video, follows Madden from coach to video game icon, and features Christian Bale, John Mulaney, Kathryn Hahn, Sienna Miller and Shane Gillis alongside Nicolas Cage as Madden, directed by David O. Russell. Mulaney plays EA Sports founder Trip Hawkins.

One more date discrepancy: E!'s piece describes the film as hitting select theaters November 18, while Wikipedia's entry cites a select-theaters date of November 11, 2026, with Prime Video streaming starting November 18, which matches Variety's August report that Amazon MGM scheduled it for Prime Video on Nov. 18 with an awards-qualifying theatrical run earlier in the month.

## Why People Are Searching For It

A 1,000+ spike late on a Saturday isn't a news event. It's a video clip getting picked up.

The sweatpants bit is the kind of thing that travels: short, funny, parent-coded, no context required. E!'s clip got republished by Yahoo and a chain of radio-station and aggregator sites within a day. People searching his name Saturday night were mostly chasing that clip, or trying to figure out whether the *Get Smart* thing is actually real.

That second group matters more. The casting news first broke in September, so a lot of people are hearing about it for the first time now, through a quote rather than a trade story.

## The Context Most Coverage Skips

*Get Smart* has been rebooted, revived, and rebooted again for 60 years, and the track record is uneven at best. Mel Brooks and Buck Henry launched the original in 1965 and it ran 138 episodes through 1970, with Don Adams as the incompetent Agent Smart, Barbara Feldon as the far more capable Agent 99, and Edward Platt as the Chief. Dark Horizons notes the series won seven Emmys, including two for Outstanding Comedy Series, and was followed by two films and a short-lived 1990s revival.

Then came 2008. Variety reports that Peter Segal's film with Steve Carell, Anne Hathaway, Dwayne Johnson, Alan Arkin and Terence Stamp opened to $38 million and finished at $230 million worldwide against an $80 million budget — profitable, but THR notes a planned sequel was eventually scrapped. That's the shape of this property: it makes money, nobody loves it enough to keep going.

The timeline on the current version is short and still soft. Deadline reported in September 2026 that Mulaney was eyeing the Maxwell Smart role at Warner Bros., with Grahame-Smith writing and Roven and Lazar producing. Variety confirmed the same and described the project as in very early stages. Two months later, Mulaney talks about it on a carpet in the present tense. That's progress, but it's the cheapest kind — an actor describing a character he likes, not a start date.

The comparison I keep coming back to is casting logic. Carell's Maxwell Smart was built around physical comedy and sincerity. Mulaney's whole register is verbal: precision, confidence, total ignorance delivered at speed. His own THR quote is basically a thesis statement for that reading. Whether a studio will finance a spy comedy that runs on timing instead of set pieces is a different question entirely.

## What We Know and What We Don't

Confirmed: Mulaney is in *Madden* as Trip Hawkins, per Amazon's own page. The Prime Video date is November 18, 2026. He spoke to both E! News and THR at the film's LA premiere in early November. He has talked publicly about the *Get Smart* role and what draws him to it.

Not confirmed: whether a deal for *Get Smart* has actually closed. Variety and Deadline both framed it in September as talks or circling, and I found nothing since announcing a signed deal, a director, or a production start. No release window exists.

Unverified details: the exact premiere date, and the theatrical date E! gives. I'd go with the Variety and Wikipedia version on the latter.

Also reported but secondary: multiple outlets citing ANI and a Borneo Post item say Mulaney has a nine-show New York run, *Mulaney Takes Manhattan*, starting in January. I couldn't verify that against a primary ticketing or venue source, so treat it as reported rather than confirmed.

## My Take

My read: this trend is a press tour working exactly as designed, and the sweatpants clip is the part that will be forgotten fastest. Amazon needs eyeballs on a David O. Russell biopic that's going straight to streaming eleven days from now, and a likable comedian giving charming quotes is cheap, effective marketing. That's not a criticism. It's just what's happening.

I think the *Get Smart* casting is genuinely smart, and I'd say that even if the project collapses. The 2008 film treated Maxwell Smart as a lovable klutz. The original character was funnier than that — a man whose confidence was completely untethered from his competence. Mulaney has spent two decades building exactly that persona on stage, and his own description of the appeal lines up with it.

The fair counterpoint: being well-cast and being able to open a movie are different skills. Carell was coming off *The Office* at peak and the 2008 film still only did $230 million globally. Mulaney's draw is a comedy-special and live-show audience, which doesn't automatically convert to a $100 million-plus studio tentpole. Warner Bros. knows this, which may be why the thing has sat in early development since September.

What I'd actually watch for: a director attachment and a production start date. Until one of those lands, this is a script and an enthusiastic actor, and Hollywood is full of both. If you're deciding where to put your attention this month, *Madden* on November 18 is the real, dated thing. *Get Smart* is a maybe with a good lead attached to it.
