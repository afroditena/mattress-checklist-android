## What freelance accounting software actually costs

![support.waveapps.com official page screenshot](https://afroditena.github.io/mattress-checklist-android/assets/img/best-accounting-software-for-freelancers-in-2026/2.png)
*Screenshot of support.waveapps.com ([source](https://support.waveapps.com/hc/en-us/articles/208621296-Is-Wave-free))*

There are three separate cost buckets, and most people only look at the first one.

**1. The subscription.** Entry-level plans for solo users generally land somewhere between $0 and about $25/month, with mid-tier plans climbing from there. Wave is the clearest case: 
the Starter Plan is free and includes basic accounting and invoicing, while the Pro Plan is a paid subscription adding receipt scanning, automatic bank transaction imports, and automatic transaction merging and categorization
. QuickBooks also has a free tier, but it's deliberately thin — 
it caps you at two invoices a month (unless you qualify for and enable QuickBooks Payments), one connected bank account, and the top three reports: profit and loss, balance sheet, and account quick report
.

**2. Payment processing.** This is where real money goes. 
On Wave's Pro Plan, card processing runs 2.9% + $0 (Visa, Mastercard, Discover) and 3.4% + $0 (Amex) for the first ten cumulative transactions in a monthly subscription period, then 2.9% + $0.60 and 3.4% + $0.60 per transaction after that
. 
Bank payments are 1%, with a minimum fee of $1
. Do the math on your own volume: on $8,000/month invoiced by card at roughly 2.9%, you're looking at about $232 in fees — more than ten times a typical subscription. Getting clients onto ACH instead of card is usually the single biggest cost lever you have.

**3. Add-ons.** Payroll, advanced payments, and extra team seats are almost always billed separately from the accounting plan, on every major platform. If you're truly solo, you can skip all of it.

## Watch the intro-pricing trap

![irs.gov official page screenshot](https://afroditena.github.io/mattress-checklist-android/assets/img/best-accounting-software-for-freelancers-in-2026/4.png)
*Screenshot of irs.gov ([source](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employment-tax-social-security-and-medicare-taxes))*

Every major vendor discounts heavily for new users, and those discounts expire. Intuit spells this out in its own terms: 
the discount applies to the monthly price for the first 3 months of service, followed by the then-current list price, and your account is automatically charged monthly until you cancel
.

Practical rule: budget the post-promo price, not the promo price. Put a calendar reminder for the day the discount ends and re-decide then. A $5/month teaser that becomes $38/month is a real $400+/year decision, and switching accounting platforms mid-year is a genuine pain (you'll be re-importing transactions and re-checking categorizations).

Because these list prices and promos change several times a year, verify the current number on the vendor's own pricing page before you subscribe — not on a review roundup.

## Who should pay — and who genuinely shouldn't

**Stay free if:** you have fewer than ~5 clients, get paid by ACH or direct deposit, have one dedicated business checking account, and file a straightforward Schedule C. A free tier plus a spreadsheet for quarterly tax set-asides is honestly fine. 
A free plan covering basic accounting and invoicing
 handles a lot.

**Pay for a plan if:** you're spending more than an hour a month manually typing in transactions, you have 20+ transactions a month, you need receipt capture for audit defense, or you want your accountant to log in directly instead of receiving a chaotic export. 
Automatic bank imports and receipt scanning are exactly what the paid tiers are selling
 — you're buying back time, not features.

**Skip accounting software entirely if:** you made under a few thousand dollars from freelancing this year and have maybe 15 expenses total. A labeled spreadsheet and a folder of receipts is enough. Don't pay $228/year to organize $2,000 of income.

## Concrete steps to pick and set up in one afternoon

1. **Count your monthly transactions and invoices.** Under 10 invoices and under 20 expenses? Start free.
2. **Check how your clients pay you.** If most pay by card through invoices, processing fees will dominate your costs — compare fee schedules, not subscription prices.
3. **Ask your tax preparer what they use.** If they live in QuickBooks, using it removes a recurring annoyance every March.
4. **Open a separate business checking account first.** Software can't untangle commingled personal and business spending, and neither can you in April.
5. **Start on the free tier or free trial and run one full month.** Send real invoices, categorize a real month of transactions. Only upgrade when a specific limit actually blocks you.
6. **Set your quarterly tax reserve inside the tool.** Self-employment tax is 15.3% of net earnings (12.4% Social Security + 2.9% Medicare), and that's on top of income tax — so most freelancers set aside somewhere in the 25–35% range depending on their bracket and state. Use your software's profit-and-loss report to estimate, and confirm the current-year rules on irs.gov.

## Alternatives worth considering

- **A spreadsheet plus your bank's CSV export.** Free, and perfectly adequate below roughly $30K/year of simple income.
- **Invoicing-only tools.** If your expenses are minimal and you just need to get paid, a standalone invoicing app may be cheaper than full accounting software.
- **A bookkeeper for a year-end cleanup.** Some freelancers do DIY on a free tier all year and pay a pro a few hundred dollars in January to reconcile and prep. Depending on rates in your area, that can cost less than a mid-tier subscription.
- **Business checking with built-in bookkeeping.** Several business banks now include categorization and tax-set-aside buckets. Verify features on the bank's own site, since these vary a lot.

## FAQ

**Is free accounting software good enough for freelancers?**
For most solo freelancers, yes. 
Wave's free Starter Plan includes basic accounting and invoicing features
. You lose automation — 
auto-import of bank transactions and receipt scanning sit on the paid tier
 — so it's a time-versus-money trade, not a capability cliff.

**What's the cheapest accounting software for freelancers in 2026?**
Free tiers exist at both Wave and QuickBooks, though QuickBooks' free version is limited — 
two invoices a month and one connected bank account
. Among low-cost paid plans, 
Wave Pro is $19 USD/month per business
.

**Do I need accounting software to file self-employment taxes?**
No. The IRS requires accurate records, not a specific product. Software just makes producing a Schedule C-ready profit-and-loss report faster and less error-prone.

**Will switching accounting software mess up my books?**
It can. Historical transactions, categories, and reconciliations don't always transfer cleanly. If you switch, do it at the start of a tax year and keep a full export of the old system.

**Are payment processing fees tax deductible?**
Merchant processing fees are generally an ordinary and necessary business expense for self-employed filers. Confirm how to report yours on irs.gov or with your tax preparer — this isn't tax advice for your specific situation.
