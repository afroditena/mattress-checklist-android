If you're taking notes for yourself and you want off Notion, **Obsidian** is the best free pick: the app is free, your notes are plain Markdown files on your own drive, and nothing important is locked behind a subscription. **Anytype** is the runner-up if you want a Notion-style database feel with local, encrypted storage. And honestly, the third option worth naming is **Notion's own free plan** — for a solo note-taker it's more generous than people assume, and leaving may be solving a problem you don't have.

- **Actually free, not a trial.** The core note-taking experience has to work indefinitely at $0, not for 14 days.
- **Real free-tier limits matter more than the headline price.** We looked at what's capped: file size, sync, history, storage.
- **Solo use, not team use.** Collaboration features, admin controls, and guest limits were ignored on purpose.
- **Data portability.** Can you get your notes out in Markdown or another open format without a paid plan?
- **Reasonable learning curve.** A tool that takes a weekend to configure has to earn that time back.

## Obsidian — the best free pick for most people

![notion.com official page screenshot](https://afroditena.github.io/mattress-checklist-android/assets/img/best-free-notion-alternatives-for-personal-notes/3.png)
*Screenshot of notion.com ([source](https://www.notion.com/pricing))*

Obsidian is a local-first Markdown editor with backlinks, a graph view, canvas boards, and a large community plugin library. It's the closest thing to "Notion without the subscription pressure" for personal notes, because the thing you download *is* the whole product.
Obsidian is free and 100% user-supported.
 
Your data is stored locally on your device, which means the company can't access it
, and 
the desktop and mobile apps have no feature limits, no ads, and are free for commercial use as well
.

**The real free-tier limits:** there aren't feature limits — there's a *convenience* limit. The free app doesn't sync across devices for you. You either roll your own (iCloud Drive, Dropbox, Syncthing, a Git repo) or pay for the official service. 
Obsidian Sync runs $4/user/month billed annually, and Obsidian Publish — which turns notes into a website — is $8/site/month annually
. 
Students, faculty, and nonprofit employees get 40% off Sync and Publish.
 Neither is required to take notes.

**Who it's for:** anyone building a long-term personal knowledge base who cares about owning the files. **Who should skip it:** people who want a polished mobile-first app that just works on day one. Obsidian rewards setup time; if you resent configuring software, you'll resent this.

## Anytype — the closest thing to Notion's structure, stored locally

Anytype is the option for people who actually liked Notion's model — objects, types, relations, databases — but not the cloud dependency. It looks and behaves more like Notion than Obsidian does, with a visual editor and structured data instead of a folder of text files.
Notes, tasks, databases, and chats stay on your device — owned, secure, and private — and it's free to start.
 
Anytype prices its remote backup and sync service rather than the app itself, which stays free and open source for local-only use; paid tiers expand network storage and collaboration.


**The real free-tier limits:** the free membership includes a capped amount of network (sync/backup) storage, and that cap is the reason most solo users eventually upgrade — check the current number on Anytype's pricing page before you commit, since it has changed. Local storage isn't the constraint; syncing large attachments across devices is.

**Who it's for:** ex-Notion users who want the database mental model plus encryption and offline access. **Who should skip it:** anyone who needs a mature plugin ecosystem or deep third-party integrations. Anytype is younger than Obsidian and it shows in the extension ecosystem.

## Notion's free plan — the baseline you should check before switching

Before migrating a few hundred notes somewhere else, be honest about whether Notion's free tier is actually failing you. For one person, it mostly isn't.
On a Free Plan workspace used by yourself, you can add as many pages and blocks as you like.
 The caps land elsewhere: 
on the Free plan, images, videos, and file attachments are capped at 5MB each
, and 
key limits include up to 10 guests and 7 days of page history
.

**The real free-tier limits:** the 5MB file ceiling is the one solo users hit first — screenshot-heavy or PDF-heavy notes will run into it fast. Seven-day history means a mistake you notice two weeks later isn't recoverable. And 
once a Free workspace has multiple members, you can only add a limited number of blocks before you have to upgrade — after that you can still read and edit existing content, but not add new blocks
.

**Who it's for:** people whose notes are mostly text, who like the databases, and who don't want to rebuild a system. **Who should leave:** anyone who stores big files, wants offline-first reliability, or is uncomfortable having their entire second brain in someone else's cloud.

## Apple Notes and Google Keep — the free apps you already have

These get dismissed as "not serious," which is a mistake for a lot of people. Apple Notes handles folders, tags, handwriting, scanning, and locked notes; Google Keep is excellent for fast capture, checklists, and reminders. Neither has a pricing page because neither has a paid tier — they're included with your Apple or Google account, and their sync runs on that account's existing storage.

**The real limits:** almost no extensibility, weak linking between notes, and export options that range from clumsy (Apple Notes) to basic (Keep). Keep in particular is a capture tool, not a knowledge base — there's no structure to grow into.

**Who it's for:** people who take 5–20 notes a week and mostly need to find them again. If that's you, installing a PKM system is overhead, not productivity.

## FAQ

**Is Obsidian really free, or is that a bait-and-switch?**
It's genuinely free. 
The core app costs $0 and includes all features, plugins, and themes.
 The paid products are Sync and Publish, which are services, not unlocked features.

**What's the easiest way to get my notes out of Notion?**
Notion exports to Markdown, and both Obsidian and Anytype can ingest Markdown. Expect cleanup: nested databases, linked views, and relations rarely survive a migration intact. Export first, spot-check a few pages, then decide.

**Do I need sync to use Obsidian on my phone?**
No, but you need *some* sync. The mobile app reads a vault in iCloud Drive or a synced folder just fine. Obsidian Sync is the paid, end-to-end encrypted convenience option — worth it if you don't want to troubleshoot conflict files.

**Which one should I pick if I just want to stop thinking about this?**
Obsidian if you want notes that outlive any company's business model; Notion's free plan if your current setup works and you were only shopping because of upgrade prompts.
