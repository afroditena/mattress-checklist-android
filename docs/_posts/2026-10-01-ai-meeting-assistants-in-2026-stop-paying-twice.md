**My verdict:** If you're already paying for Zoom, Google Workspace, or Microsoft 365, a separate AI meeting assistant is probably a duplicate bill — check what's already switched on in your account before you buy anything. The standalone tools earn their money in one specific situation: you hop between platforms (a Zoom call, then a Google Meet, then a client's Teams link) and you need every one of those in a single searchable archive. I'm leading with that because the buying question here isn't "is AI note-taking good yet" — it mostly is — it's "am I about to pay $10–20 a seat for something my employer already bought."

## What's Actually Going On

![guide.fireflies.ai official page screenshot](https://afroditena.github.io/mattress-checklist-android/assets/img/ai-meeting-assistants-in-2026-stop-paying-twice/2.png)
*Screenshot of guide.fireflies.ai ([source](https://guide.fireflies.ai/articles/3734844560-learn-about-the-fireflies-pricing-plans))*

An AI meeting assistant does roughly four things: joins or records the call, transcribes it, writes a summary, and pulls out action items. Some also let you search across every meeting you've ever had, which is the feature people underrate.

There are two flavors, and they're priced completely differently.

**Built into the platform.** 
Zoom folded its AI Companion into paid accounts at no additional cost, which it announced back in September 2023
 — notable at the time because the rest of the industry was charging a per-seat AI surcharge. 
Zoom spread it across Meetings, Team Chat, Phone, Email, and Whiteboard
. Google and Microsoft have their own equivalents inside Workspace and Teams. The catch with all of these: your admin controls whether it's on, and on Zoom's side you need a paid license, not the free tier.

**Standalone bots.** Fireflies, Otter, Fathom and friends. These join your calendar invites as a participant, so they work the same whether the meeting is on Zoom, Meet, or Teams. 
Fireflies lists a free tier, Pro at $10 per user per month billed annually or $18 billed monthly, Business at $19 annually or $29 monthly, and Enterprise at $39 per user annually
. 
Its Pro tier includes unlimited transcription, unlimited AI summaries, 8,000 minutes of storage per seat, and 20 AI credits
.

That "AI credits" line is the part worth staring at. Transcription is unlimited; the fancier AI actions are metered. I'd read any vendor's credit definition carefully before assuming "unlimited" covers what you want to do.

Otter is the other name everyone brings up. I saw a lot of consistent third-party reporting on its tiers and minute caps, but I couldn't verify those numbers on Otter's own pricing page while writing this, so I'm not going to print them.

## Where It Breaks

![news.zoom.com official page screenshot](https://afroditena.github.io/mattress-checklist-android/assets/img/ai-meeting-assistants-in-2026-stop-paying-twice/3.png)
*Screenshot of news.zoom.com ([source](https://news.zoom.com/zoom-ai-companion/))*

- **Crosstalk and accents.** Transcripts degrade when three people talk at once or someone's on a bad connection. The summary then confidently paraphrases something nobody said.
- **It can't read the room.** An AI will record "team agreed to move the deadline" and miss that two people agreed through gritted teeth.
- **Action items get attributed to the wrong person.** Especially when someone says "yeah, I can take that" without naming themselves.
- **Metered features sneak up on you.** Storage caps and credit pools, not the headline price, are what decide your real bill — 
Fireflies' Pro storage is 8,000 minutes per seat
, which sounds huge until you're recording six hours a week for a year.
- **Bots in the participant list are socially weird.** A named bot joining a client call changes the tone of that call. Some clients will ask you to remove it.
- **Admin lockouts.** If you're on a company Workspace or Zoom account, IT may have disabled AI features or blocked external bots entirely. That's not a bug you can fix from your side.

## The Consent Part (I'm Not a Lawyer)

Recording law in the US varies by state — some require only one party's consent, others require everyone's. I'm not a lawyer and this isn't legal advice, so check your own state and your employer's policy rather than taking a blog's word for it.

The practical rule I'd follow regardless of law: say out loud that it's recording, at the start, every time. Not because it's required everywhere, but because the trust cost of someone discovering a transcript later is far higher than the four seconds it takes to announce it.

One more thing to check: whether your vendor uses your meeting audio to train models, and whether that's on by default. This varies per vendor and per plan, and it's the single setting I'd look at before uploading anything sensitive.

## Built-In vs. Standalone, Quickly

| | Built into your platform | Standalone assistant |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Bundled with paid plans (Zoom includes AI Companion at no extra cost on paid accounts) | Roughly $10–$39/user/month depending on tier and billing (Fireflies' published range) |
| Works across Zoom + Meet + Teams | No | Yes |
| Shows up as a visible bot | No | Usually |
| Who controls it | Your IT admin | You |

## What I Learned While Writing This (and What I Think)

Three things stood out. First, how much the pricing conversation has moved from "price per seat" to "price per credit" — vendors advertise unlimited transcription while metering the AI features you actually wanted. Second, Zoom's decision to bundle AI at no extra cost is now three years old, which reframes the whole category: the standalone tools have spent that time competing against free. Third, the annual-vs-monthly gap is brutal. 
Fireflies Pro is $10 a month on annual billing and $18 month-to-month
 — nearly double for the flexibility.

My take: for a solo freelancer or a small team living inside one platform, a standalone subscription is a bad buy in 2026. I think that because the built-in versions now handle the 80% case — summary, action items, transcript — and you've already paid for them. The exception is real, though: if you're an agency, recruiter, or consultant bouncing between four clients' meeting platforms, one searchable archive across all of them is worth $10 a seat, and no built-in tool gives you that.

Honest limits on this piece: I read vendor pricing pages and announcements. I did not run these tools side by side in live meetings, so I can't tell you whose transcript is more accurate on a noisy call — anyone who claims a clean accuracy ranking without published methodology is guessing. Prices and credit limits also move, so confirm on the vendor's page before you put in a card.
