# Best Free Slack Alternatives for Small Team Chat

If you want a free Slack replacement that won't quietly hide your old messages, **Pumble** is the best pick for most small teams — its free plan includes unlimited message history and unlimited users. **Zulip Cloud Free** is the runner-up if your team likes organized, threaded discussions, and **Discord** is a solid third option for small, informal teams that live in voice chat.

The reason people go looking in the first place is usually the same: 
Slack's free plan shows only the most recent 90 days of messages and files, limits huddles to 1:1, and caps you at 10 connected apps
. 
That history window is what pushes most teams to upgrade — it doesn't stop you from working, it just stops you from looking backward
. And 
Slack Pro runs $7.25 per user per month billed annually, or $8.75 month to month
, which adds up fast even on a team of eight.

## Pumble — Best Overall Free Slack Alternative

![support.discord.com official page screenshot](https://afroditena.github.io/mattress-checklist-android/assets/img/best-free-slack-alternatives-for-small-team-chat/3.png)
*Screenshot of support.discord.com ([source](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000435108-What-are-Nitro-Nitro-Basic))*

Pumble, made by CAKE.com, is the closest thing to a like-for-like Slack swap on a free plan. The layout is familiar enough that most people won't need training: channels down the left, threads, DMs, reactions, and search.

The headline is history. 
Pumble's Free plan includes unlimited users, channels, and message history
, and 
Pumble describes the free plan as having no limitations, positioning it for startups and non-profits
. That said, "no limitations" is marketing language — in practice, users report that 
file-sharing limits on the free plan can feel restrictive when working with larger files
, and the more advanced admin, guest, and integration controls live on the paid tiers.

**Free-tier reality check:** Unlimited people and unlimited searchable history are the wins. If you need bigger file uploads, richer permissions, or longer video meeting features, you're looking at upgrading. 
Paid plans start with annual billing (or $4.99/user/month if billed monthly)
, with 
Pumble pricing in 2026 ranging from $2.49 to $6.99 per user depending on the plan
 — still well under Slack Pro.

**Best for:** Small businesses and remote teams that want Slack's workflow without the 90-day memory loss.

## Zulip Cloud — Best for Teams Drowning in Channel Noise

Zulip's differentiator is its conversation model. Instead of one long channel feed, every message belongs to a named topic inside a channel, so you can catch up on exactly the threads that matter and skip the rest. For distributed teams working across time zones, that structure beats scroll-and-hope.

Zulip is also 
100% open source, and organizations that don't need support can self-host it for free with no limitations
 — useful if you have someone technical and want full control of your data.

**Free-tier reality check:** The cloud version caps history by volume, not by time. 
Only the most recent 10,000 messages can be viewed and searched on the Zulip Cloud Free plan; older messages are still stored and become available again if you upgrade to Zulip Cloud Standard
. Zulip is explicit that 
it won't switch to a 90-day style limit, arguing a time-based cap is a bad model for a collaboration tool
. Note the math: a chatty eight-person team can burn through 10,000 messages in a couple of months, while a quieter team might take a year.

One more perk worth knowing: 
Zulip sponsors over 1,500 organizations
, and 
sponsorships and special education pricing are available for qualifying groups
 — so nonprofits, open-source projects, and research teams should apply before paying anything.

**Best for:** Teams that do a lot of async, decision-heavy discussion and want it organized by topic.

## Discord — Best Free Voice and Screen Sharing

Discord gets dismissed as a gaming app, but for a five- to fifteen-person team it's a genuinely capable free workspace: persistent text channels, always-on voice rooms you can drop into, screen sharing, and no per-seat billing at all. 
Voice calls, video, text messaging, servers, and friend lists are all free with no time limits or membership requirements
, and 
the free tier covers unlimited messages, voice channels, video calls up to 25 people, and communities of any size
.

**Free-tier reality check:** The pinch point is file size. 
The free plan allows uploads up to 10MB
, which means design files, video, and big PDFs all end up in Google Drive or Dropbox anyway. Paid upgrades are individual, not organizational: 
Nitro Basic adds 50MB uploads plus custom emoji and stickers
, and 
Nitro is the full premium tier with HD video streaming and two Server Boosts
. The other caveat is governance — 
Discord isn't built for enterprise-style admin and compliance controls
, so it's a poor fit if you handle regulated or client-confidential data.

**Best for:** Small, informal teams — agencies, studios, side projects — that want spontaneous voice chat more than audit logs.

## FAQ

**Does Slack's free plan actually delete my old messages?**
No — they're hidden, not erased. 
After 90 days, older messages are hidden but not deleted
, and they come back if you upgrade. That's worth knowing if you're on the fence: your history isn't gone, it's just locked behind a paywall.

**Is there a user limit on Slack's free plan?**
No. 
Slack's free plan has no user limit — the restrictions are on features, not headcount
. So "too many people" is never the trigger to upgrade; needing old messages is.

**Which free alternative is easiest to migrate to?**
Pumble, by a wide margin. The channel-and-thread structure mirrors Slack closely enough that most teams adapt in a day. Zulip takes longer because the topic model genuinely changes how you write messages — that's the point, but budget a week of adjustment.

**Can I use these for client work with sensitive data?**
Be careful. Discord in particular 
isn't designed for enterprise governance
, so avoid it for anything regulated. If data control is the priority, 
Zulip's open-source, self-hostable option
 gives you the most say over where your messages live — provided you have someone who can run a server.

**The short version:** if the 90-day wall is your only real complaint about Slack, Pumble solves it for free. If your problem is channel chaos rather than cost, Zulip's topic threading is worth the learning curve. And if your team mostly talks out loud, Discord handles that better than any free business chat tool.
