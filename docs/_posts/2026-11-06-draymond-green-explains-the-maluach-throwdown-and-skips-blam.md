Three days after he picked a 7-foot-1 rookie up off the floor and put him back down on it, Draymond Green went on his own podcast and explained himself. Not apologized — explained. That Thursday episode, November 5, 2026, is what pushed his name back into US Google Search.

## What's Happening

![nba.com official page screenshot](https://afroditena.github.io/mattress-checklist-android/assets/img/draymond-green-explains-the-maluach-throwdown-and-skips-blam/2.png)
*Screenshot of nba.com ([source](https://www.nba.com/player/203110/draymond-green))*

The incident happened Monday, November 2, at Chase Center in San Francisco. According to Arizona Sports, Green was ejected during the third quarter after grabbing Suns center Khaman Maluach and throwing him to the floor, which prompted Dillon Brooks to run over and get in Green's face. Green was whistled for a foul, then assessed a technical for shoving Maluach down; the ensuing altercation produced double technicals, and the second tech ejected him.

Yahoo Sports reported that the sequence began when Green and Maluach got tangled in the lane during Golden State's 116-113 win, and that Brooks also picked up a technical for his part. Fox News' Outkick described Brooks putting a hand on Green to back him off Maluach, Green grabbing Brooks by the jersey, and Green having to be pulled toward the sideline.

Green finished with four points, four rebounds, two assists and a steal in 17 minutes. The Warriors trailed 67-57 when he was tossed, came back to win, and moved to 5-3.

Then came the part that actually spiked the search term. On his podcast, Green rejected Brooks' postgame claim that he was trying to "de-escalate," per Bleacher Report, telling him "You were trying to be the villain" and saying that kind of energy would never be respected.

On the throwdown itself, Green's reasoning was about reputation. "You must stand on that as a big man, if you don't, your paint get ran through, no rim protection cause there's no respect after that," he said in comments reported by Yardbarker.

## Why People Are Searching For It

The game was Monday. The surge was Thursday night. That gap tells you the search isn't really about the ejection — it's about the explanation.

Green hosts a podcast, so he gets a second news cycle on his own incidents that other players don't get. He used it to both justify the foul and attack Brooks' version of events. That's two separate quotable fights in one episode, which is why the clips circulated.

Who's searching: Warriors fans checking whether he'd miss Thursday's game, Suns fans wanting the Brooks counterpunch, and the much larger group of people who follow Draymond Green the way you follow a long-running show rather than a basketball player.

## The Context Most Coverage Skips

Start with something nobody seems to agree on: how many times this has happened. Yahoo Sports called it his 23rd regular-season ejection. UPI, the same week, called it the 31st of his career. Different counting rules — regular season only versus everything, possibly including preseason — but the gap is wide enough that you should treat any single number you see as approximate unless the league itself publishes it.

Second piece of missing context: the discipline. ESPN's Shams Charania said on The Pat McAfee Show that he did not expect the NBA to discipline Green any further. That's a report of an expectation, not a league announcement — one Yahoo write-up was careful to say exactly that, noting Charania's comments describe his expectation and are not a formal NBA announcement. Useful distinction, and most headlines flattened it into "NBA makes decision."

Third: the stakes if it had gone the other way. A one-game suspension would have cost Green Thursday's home game against Giannis Antetokounmpo and the Miami Heat. He played instead.

And the comparison point that makes this feel smaller than the reaction suggests: Green once struck Phoenix center Jusuf Nurkic in the face, drew a flagrant 2 and an ejection, and missed 12 games before reinstatement. Measured against that, Monday was a hard, ugly foul plus a jersey grab. The league apparently graded it that way too.

Last thing: Maluach's own reaction undercut the outrage. He brushed it off, saying "Nothing against Draymond, he's just playing basketball out there." The 20-year-old who got thrown down was the calmest person involved.

## What We Know and What We Don't

Confirmed: Green threw Maluach to the floor early in the third quarter on November 2 at Chase Center, got a technical for it, got a second for the Brooks scuffle, and was ejected. Brooks got one technical. Golden State won 116-113 after trailing. Green defended the foul and attacked Brooks' account on his podcast on November 5.

Also reported but worth labeling as a quote rather than a fact: Brooks told reporters he was "just trying to de-escalate" and claimed Green "was just tweaking all game," according to ESPN via People. Those are competing characterizations of the same ten seconds, and the video doesn't settle intent.

Open: whether the NBA issued any fine. Charania's reporting covered suspension; I didn't find a confirmed league statement on a monetary penalty either way, so I'm not going to claim one exists. Also open is whether Green's on-court technical count this season trends anywhere problematic — it's early, and he has one ejection.

Still developing: the next Warriors–Suns meeting. Nobody needs a prediction for how that one's officiated.

## My Take

My read is that Green's explanation is more honest than it is defensible, and that's what makes it interesting. He didn't say he lost his temper. He said a big man who gets knocked down and scored on loses the paint for the rest of the night, and that the play "can't end like that." That's a coherent theory of deterrence. It's also exactly the logic the league spent a decade legislating out of the game, which is why the technical was correct.

I think the Brooks half is the weaker argument. Green's objection is that Brooks went to the media claiming peacemaker duty after physically inserting himself — and honestly, that's fair. Bleacher Report made the same point from the other direction: Brooks' reputation precedes him, and when he enters a pile, nobody assumes he's calming it down. But Green complaining that someone else is performing villainy for the cameras, on his own podcast, is a hard position to hold with a straight face.

The fair point for the other side: Green's version of the contact is contested. Maluach said he made no illegal contact beforehand, and at least one account described Green as flopping for a charge before getting up and throwing him down. If that's what the tape shows, the "respect" framing is retroactive justification for a bad reaction.

What a reasonable reader should watch: whether the no-further-discipline outcome becomes a pattern. Green is 36, he's a defensive anchor on a team that sits at 5-3, and the Warriors clearly got a jolt out of this one. The risk isn't this foul. It's that the lesson learned was "this works."
