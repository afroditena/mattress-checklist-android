308 points in eight games. That's the number that pushed "luka doncic stats" into US trending search on Friday night, November 6, 2026, and it's the number worth sitting with, because the last person to do it was Michael Jordan in 1988-89.

Dončić is nine games into his first full season as the Lakers' undisputed center of gravity, and the box scores have stopped looking normal. Below is what's actually confirmed, what's still in progress, and why the comparison everyone reaches for — Jordan — is both correct and slightly misleading.

## What's Happening

![nba.com official page screenshot](https://afroditena.github.io/mattress-checklist-android/assets/img/luka-dončićs-385-ppg-start-explained-by-the-numbers/2.png)
*Screenshot of nba.com ([source](https://www.nba.com/stats/player/1629029))*

According to the Associated Press report carried by CBS Sports, Dončić scored 40 points in a 116-103 win at Sacramento on Wednesday, November 4, hitting six 3-pointers and 12 of 13 free throws while adding nine rebounds and seven assists.

The same report notes he had played eight of the Lakers' nine games and scored 308 points — the first player past 300 through his first eight games of a season since Jordan's 303 in 1988-89. Only Wilt Chamberlain has ever scored more in that span, topping out at 418 in 1962-63.

Per NBA.com's Starting 5 newsletter, the Sacramento game also gave him six straight games with 35-plus points, five-plus rebounds, five-plus assists and five-plus 3-pointers — the longest such streak in NBA history. Basketnews, citing the Lakers, reported the same streak as an outright first and noted it came on Dončić's 100th game as a Laker.

The November 1 game against Toronto was the loudest one. ESPN's recap credits him with 45 points, a season-high 15 rebounds, six assists and five steals in a 131-125 win, with eight 3-pointers. NBA.com called it the first 45-point, 15-rebound, 5-assist, 5-steal, 5-three game in league history.

The next night, per ESPN, he put up 42 points, 13 assists and seven rebounds in a 138-117 win over Miami.

Entering Friday's NBA Cup group game against Portland, ESPN's season leaders page listed him at 38.5 points and 8.0 assists per game on 49.5% shooting and 89.3% from the line, with the Lakers 7-2.

## Why People Are Searching For It

Two things stacked on the same night. The historical markers hit the wires on November 5 — Sky Sports framed it as the best eight-game points total since Jordan 38 years ago — and then Dončić played a nationally streamed NBA Cup game on Friday evening Pacific time, which is exactly when the search term spiked.

The searchers split into obvious camps: fantasy managers checking a per-game line, casual fans who saw a highlight clip and wanted to know if the pace is real, and bettors looking at points props. "Stats" searches almost always mean someone wants the raw number, not the narrative.

## The Context Most Coverage Skips

The Jordan comparison is accurate and still undersells the structural weirdness here.

Jordan's 1986-87 and 1988-89 starts came in an era with more possessions per game and no defensive three-second rule — but also no legal zone, meaning Jordan was isolated and Dončić gets swarmed. What's different is the shape of the production. Basketnews noted Dončić's 38.5 sits just ahead of Jordan's 38.4-point start in 1986-87, and he's doing it while NBA.com pegs him at 8.1 assists per game, third-most in the league, directly accounting for 57.9 points per game through scoring and assists. Jordan in 1986-87 was not a primary creator in that way. The Athletic's framing, flagged in NBA.com's newsletter — is Luka the Jokić of guards — is closer to the truth than the Jordan frame.

The timeline matters too. NBA.com's season piece opens on the fact that this is the Lakers' first season after LeBron James' departure. Dončić opened with 92 points across the first two games, including 49 with 11 rebounds and eight assists against Minnesota, then became the first player in league history to open a season with back-to-back 40-point, 10-rebound, 5-assist games. He added 52 at Utah on October 25.

Here's the part that gets skipped: this is not a one-man heave-ho offense. NBA.com reported the Lakers piled up 39 assists against Miami, their most in a game since JJ Redick took over in 2024, and lead the league in team field goal percentage, with new center Walker Kessler the NBA's most accurate individual shooter. The gravity is converting into easy shots for other people. That's the sustainability argument, not the 40-point nights.

One more thing the "historic start" stories mostly leave out: Portland. The Blazers won in Los Angeles 136-129 on October 27 behind a balanced attack, and came into Friday at 6-1. A 38.5 scoring average has not been enough against them.

## What We Know and What We Don't

Confirmed: the 308 points through eight games, the record six-game 35/5/5/5 streak, the Toronto and Miami lines, the 7-2 record entering Friday, and the per-game averages above.

Not confirmed: the final score of Friday's Lakers-Blazers NBA Cup game. ESPN's box score was still live when I checked, showing Portland ahead 60-53 in the third quarter, with CBS Sports listing Dončić at 17 points, two rebounds and two assists at that point. I'm not going to guess at a result I didn't verify.

Also unverified in detail: ESPN published a piece on Dončić discussing his own improved defense after the Raptors win. I didn't confirm the quotes inside it. The one hard defensive datapoint I did verify is the five-steal game against Toronto, which ESPN noted was the second time he's hit 40 points, 10 rebounds and five steals as a Laker, a line no Laker had posted since 1982-83.

Open question: the pace. Nobody has averaged 38 over a full season since 1962-63. He also missed one of the Lakers' first nine games.

## My Take

I think the scoring average is the least interesting number in this stretch, and it's the only one anybody's quoting.

My read: the 39-assist night against Miami tells you more about whether the Lakers are good than the 45-point night against Toronto does. Teams are sending bodies at Dončić, and NBA.com's reporting shows the counter is already working — passes out of pressure, league-leading team field goal percentage, Kessler finishing lobs. Historic individual scoring with a bad supporting cast is a 44-win season. This looks like something else.

The fair counterpoint: Portland beat them 136-129 in late October with the exact formula that beats Luka teams — outscore the one-man engine with five guys. Portland came into Friday at 6-1. A great offense that can't get stops is a familiar Lakers problem, and nine games is a tiny sample.

What I'd watch instead of the PPG: the Lakers' assist totals and their defensive rating over the next month, which includes a four-game road swing through Detroit, Atlanta and Milwaukee in mid-November. If the assists hold while the scoring average inevitably drops to the low 30s, that's a contender. If the scoring drops and nothing replaces it, 38.5 was a hot month.

And I'd stop treating the Jordan line as the headline. Jordan never averaged 38 and 8 assists. The closer historical rhyme is Oscar Robertson or prime Jokić — a player whose counting stats look absurd because he's the offense's operating system, not just its finisher.
