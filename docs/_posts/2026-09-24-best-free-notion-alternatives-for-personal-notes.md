If you just want a free place to keep your personal notes and you're done wrestling with Notion, **Obsidian** is the best pick for most people: the full app is free, your notes live on your own machine as plain Markdown files, and nothing important is paywalled. **Joplin** is the runner-up if you want open-source software with a built-in web clipper, and **Google Keep** (or **Apple Notes**, if you're all-in on Apple) is the right answer if you mostly need quick capture instead of a knowledge system.

Worth saying up front: Notion's free plan is genuinely fine for solo note-taking. 
Notion's Free plan includes unlimited blocks for a single member, up to 10 external guests, 5 MB per file upload, 7-day page history, and trial AI capabilities.
 The usual reasons people leave anyway are speed, offline reliability, the 5 MB file cap, and not wanting their notes locked inside someone else's database format.

## How We Picked

- **Actually free, not a trial.** The core note-taking experience has to work indefinitely at $0, with no credit card and no countdown.
- **Real free-tier limits, stated plainly.** We looked for the thing that bites later: file size caps, sync fees, device limits.
- **Good for one person.** These are personal-notes picks. Team wikis and real-time collaboration are a different shopping list.
- **Your notes stay portable.** Plain text, Markdown, or an easy export beats a proprietary format you can't get out of.
- **Low setup cost.** If it takes a weekend of plugin tinkering just to write a grocery list, it's not a Notion replacement for most readers.

## Obsidian — best overall free Notion alternative

![joplinapp.org official page screenshot](https://afroditena.github.io/mattress-checklist-android/assets/img/best-free-notion-alternatives-for-personal-notes/3.png)
*Screenshot of joplinapp.org ([source](https://joplinapp.org/plans/))*

Obsidian stores your notes as Markdown files in a folder on your computer, which means you can read, back up, or move them without the app. 
Obsidian is free and 100% user-supported: you can use the apps without sharing personal information, your data is stored locally on your device so it's inaccessible to the company, the apps don't collect telemetry, and user data is never sold.


**What it's good for:** building a personal knowledge base over years. Linked notes, a graph view, daily notes, tags, and a deep community plugin ecosystem. It's also fast and works entirely offline, which is where Notion tends to frustrate people.

**The real free-tier limit:** the free version doesn't include official cloud sync. 
Obsidian Sync secures your data with AES-256 end-to-end encryption, which prevents the company from reading it
 — but it's a paid add-on, as is Obsidian Publish for turning notes into a website. 
Students, faculty, and nonprofit employees are eligible for a 40% discount on Sync and Publish.
 If you don't want to pay, you can keep your vault in a folder that's already synced by another service you use, or just keep it on one device. Check the pricing page for current Sync and Publish rates before you buy.

**Skip it if:** you want databases with relations and rollups the way Notion does them, or you want something that works perfectly with zero configuration.

## Joplin — best open-source pick with a web clipper

Joplin is an open-source, Markdown-based note app for desktop, mobile, and even the terminal. It's built around notebooks, tags, attachments, and a browser clipper, which makes it a comfortable landing spot for anyone coming from Evernote-style organization rather than Notion-style databases.

**What it's good for:** research and clipping. Save articles and screenshots into notes, tag them, search them later, and keep end-to-end encryption on if you sync.

**The real free-tier limit:** the apps are free, but the company's own hosted sync service is not. 
Joplin Cloud syncs notes automatically to all your devices and adds features like publishing a note to the internet to share with others
, and 
the plans page lists no free cloud tier — the free path is running the app locally or syncing through Dropbox, OneDrive, WebDAV, or your own server
. Joplin Cloud is priced in euros, so check the plans page for current rates. One more honest caveat: Joplin isn't built for backlink-style knowledge graphs the way Obsidian is.

**Skip it if:** you want a polished, modern editor experience above all else.

## Google Keep — best for fast capture on any device

Keep is free with a Google account, runs in the browser and on iOS and Android, and syncs without you thinking about it. Notes are color-coded cards with checklists, labels, reminders, image capture, and voice notes that get transcribed.

**What it's good for:** grocery lists, quick ideas, screenshots of things you'll deal with later, and anything you need to jot down in under five seconds.

**The real limit:** it's deliberately shallow. There's no linking between notes, no folders beyond labels, no Markdown, and no database-style structure. Attachments count against your Google account storage, which is shared with Gmail and Drive. It's a capture tool, not a second brain — plenty of people run Keep for quick notes and Obsidian for everything that matters.

## Apple Notes — best free default if you're on iPhone and Mac

If your devices are all Apple, Notes is already installed, already synced via iCloud, and genuinely good now: folders, tags, smart folders, scanning documents with the camera, handwriting search, locked notes, and solid full-text search.

**What it's good for:** zero-setup personal notes with excellent search and capture, especially if you use an iPhone all day.

**The real limit:** it's an Apple ecosystem tool. The web version at iCloud.com is workable but limited, there's no real Android app, and attachments eat into your iCloud storage — Apple's free tier is small (5 GB shared across backups, photos, and files), so heavy users end up paying for iCloud+. Export options are also thinner than Markdown-based apps, so getting thousands of notes out later isn't as clean.

## FAQ

**Is Notion's free plan actually limited for one person?**
Not much. 
The Free plan has no time limit and no credit card requirement, and solo users get unlimited pages and blocks.
 The friction shows up with big file uploads, short version history, and collaboration — which is exactly when people start shopping around.

**Can I move my Notion notes to these apps?**
Notion exports to Markdown and CSV, which imports cleanly enough into Obsidian and Joplin. Expect to fix links and reformat databases by hand — databases are the part that doesn't translate, since none of these tools reproduce Notion's relational views.

**What's the cheapest way to sync Obsidian for free?**
Put your vault inside a folder that's already synced by a cloud drive you use, or use a third-party community sync plugin. It works, but it's on you to avoid edit conflicts; the paid first-party Sync exists mostly to remove that headache.

**Which one should I pick if I can't decide?**
Start with Obsidian if you want notes that link together and outlive any one app. Start with Keep or Apple Notes if your real problem is that you never write things down in the first place.
