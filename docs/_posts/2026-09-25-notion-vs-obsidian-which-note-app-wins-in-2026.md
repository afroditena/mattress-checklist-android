# Notion vs Obsidian: Which Note-Taking App Should You Choose?

**Short verdict:** Pick **Notion** if you want an all-in-one workspace with databases, shared pages, and real-time team collaboration in the cloud. Pick **Obsidian** if you want fast, local Markdown files you own outright, deep linking between notes, and a plugin ecosystem you can bend to your workflow. Most solo thinkers and writers will be happier in Obsidian; most teams, freelancers with clients, and anyone who wants structure without tinkering will be happier in Notion.

## Pricing: Two Very Different Models

![obsidian.md official page screenshot](https://afroditena.github.io/mattress-checklist-android/assets/img/notion-vs-obsidian-which-note-app-wins-in-2026/3.png)
*Screenshot of obsidian.md ([source](https://obsidian.md/sync))*

Notion charges **per seat, per month**. That's fine at one or two people and adds up fast at ten. 
Notion costs $10/user/month for Plus (annual) and $20/user/month for Business (annual), and as of May 2025 the separate AI add-on is gone — full AI access, including AI Agents and Ask Notion, now requires the Business plan.
 If AI is the reason you're shopping, that's the real number to budget against, not the $10 headline.

Notion's free plan is genuinely usable solo. The catches show up in two places: 
the 5 MB file limit, which you'll hit quickly if you embed screenshots or store PDFs, and the 7-day version history, which means a mistake from eight days ago isn't recoverable
. 
Adding a second member to a free workspace also puts the block allowance into a limited mode.


Obsidian flips the model. 
The app itself is free, including for work use — the company sells an optional $50/user/year commercial license, but it's voluntary rather than a gate.
 
The only things you can pay for are additive: Sync, Publish, a Catalyst membership, and that commercial license — none of them unlock functionality inside the editor, since plugins, themes, graph view, and canvas are all free.
 
Catalyst membership gets you early access to beta versions and community badges.


**Bottom line:** for one person, Obsidian is cheaper (often $0–$4/month). For a five-person team that needs one shared space, Notion Plus at $10/seat is the more realistic comparison — and Obsidian genuinely isn't built for that job.

## Ease of Use and the Learning Curve

![help.obsidian.md official page screenshot](https://afroditena.github.io/mattress-checklist-android/assets/img/notion-vs-obsidian-which-note-app-wins-in-2026/4.png)
*Screenshot of help.obsidian.md ([source](https://help.obsidian.md/Frequently+Asked+Questions))*

Notion is easier on day one. You open a template, type, drag blocks around, and you have something that looks polished enough to send a client. The friction comes later, when your workspace sprawls into forty half-finished pages and you're fighting relation properties in a database you set up six months ago.

Obsidian is the opposite curve: awkward on day one, smooth on day ninety. You start with a folder of Markdown files, and that's it. There's no default structure, which is either liberating or paralyzing depending on your temperament. The payoff is speed and durability — you're editing text files, so nothing loads, and nothing is trapped in a proprietary format.

One practical consideration: Notion requires a connection for most work, while 
Obsidian is designed to work without an internet connection, so your files are available on a flight or in a remote spot, and Sync merges changes when you're back online
.

## The Real Feature Split: Databases vs. Linked Notes

This is the dimension that should decide it for you.

**Notion's superpower is the database.** A Notion page can be a row in a table that's also a Kanban board that's also a calendar. If you run client projects, track invoices, maintain a content pipeline, or manage a reading list with statuses and filters, Notion does this natively and does it well. Nothing in Obsidian matches it without plugins and effort.

**Obsidian's superpower is connection.** Internal links, backlinks, and a graph view let ideas find each other over time. For a researcher, grad student, or writer building an argument across hundreds of notes, this is worth more than any Kanban board. Search is instant because everything is local text.

There's also a data-ownership angle that matters more than it used to. 
With local Markdown files, there's no server reading your notes and no risk that a company folding takes your data with it.
 Notion has export options, but exported databases lose much of what made them useful.

## Collaboration, Sync, and Sharing

Notion wins here, clearly. Real-time multiplayer editing, comments, permissions, guest access, and shared teamspaces are the product, not an add-on. 
Billing scales with the number of members in your workspace, recalculated at each renewal
 — so plan for that as your team grows.

Obsidian's sync is solid but single-player by design. 
Sync secures your data with AES-256 encryption and works across Mac, Windows, Linux, iOS, and Android.
 
Version history retention depends on your plan: 1 month on Standard, 12 months on Plus.
 Shared vaults exist, but they're not a substitute for live co-editing.

You can also skip Sync entirely and use iCloud, Dropbox, or Git — though 
those routes work fine on desktop, get fiddly on mobile, and none are end-to-end encrypted the way Obsidian Sync is
. One warning worth heeding: 
"files on-demand" features in OneDrive and Dropbox can look like deletions to Obsidian Sync, so disable on-demand downloads if you combine them
.

## Who Should Use Which

- **Freelancer juggling clients and invoices** → **Notion.** Databases, client-facing shared pages, and guest access do real work here.
- **Small team (2–15 people)** → **Notion Plus.** It's the least painful shared brain for the money.
- **Student or researcher** → **Obsidian.** Linked notes plus the education discount on Sync is hard to beat.
- **Writer building a long-term idea library** → **Obsidian.** Local files age better than any SaaS workspace.
- **Privacy- or longevity-minded user** → **Obsidian.** You control the folder.
- **Someone who wants templates and structure without configuring anything** → **Notion.**
- **Heavy AI user** → **Notion**, but budget for Business at $20/seat, not Plus.
- **Honestly? Plenty of people run both** — Notion for client-facing and team work, Obsidian for private thinking. It's less redundant than it sounds.

## FAQ

**Is Obsidian really free for work use?**
Yes. 
The old requirement for a paid commercial license is gone — you can use Obsidian for your job at no cost, though the optional $50/user/year license exists for organizations that want to support the team.


**Can I move my notes out of Notion into Obsidian?**
You can export Notion pages to Markdown and import them into an Obsidian vault. Plain pages transfer reasonably well; complex databases with relations, rollups, and views generally don't survive the trip intact. Test with a small section before committing.

**Do I have to pay for Obsidian Sync?**
No. Sync is a convenience service, and you can keep a vault in sync using cloud storage or Git instead. Just be aware of the mobile friction and encryption trade-offs noted above.

**Which is better for mobile note capture?**
Notion's mobile app is the more forgiving quick-capture tool for most people, especially for dropping in images or files. Obsidian's mobile app is capable but assumes you've already thought about how your vault is organized.
