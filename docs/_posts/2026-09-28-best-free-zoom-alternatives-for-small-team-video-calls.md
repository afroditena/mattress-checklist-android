If you're a small team that just wants reliable video calls without paying Zoom, **Google Meet** is the best free pick for most people: it runs in the browser, works with the Google Calendar you're probably already using, and its free tier is more generous on time than Zoom's. The two runners-up: **Jitsi Meet**, which has no meeting time limit at all and doesn't even require an account, and **Microsoft Teams Free**, which makes sense if your team already lives in Microsoft 365 and wants chat plus calls in one place.

For context on what you're replacing: 
Zoom's free plan ends group meetings automatically at the 40-minute mark
. That's the number every alternative below is measured against.

## How We Picked

![jitsi.org official page screenshot](https://afroditena.github.io/mattress-checklist-android/assets/img/best-free-zoom-alternatives-for-small-team-video-calls/2.png)
*Screenshot of jitsi.org ([source](https://jitsi.org/jitsi-meet/))*

- **Real free tier, not a trial.** Every tool here has a permanent free plan, not a 14-day demo that turns into a credit card prompt.
- **Small-team scale.** We cared about 3–10 person calls, not 500-person webinars. Participant caps above 100 were irrelevant to the ranking.
- **Time limit first.** The single most annoying free-plan restriction is the call cutting off mid-sentence, so meeting length carried the most weight.
- **Low friction to join.** Browser-based joining, no forced downloads, and easy calendar integration mattered more than feature checklists.
- **Screen sharing and chat included free.** Anything that paywalled basic screen share was cut.

## Google Meet — Best Overall Free Zoom Alternative

![microsoft.com official page screenshot](https://afroditena.github.io/mattress-checklist-android/assets/img/best-free-zoom-alternatives-for-small-team-video-calls/3.png)
*Screenshot of microsoft.com ([source](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-teams/free))*

Google Meet is the default answer for most small teams, mainly because the free tier is unusually usable. 
Google Meet's free plan includes 60-minute meetings, up to 100 participants, screen sharing, and live captions.
 That's 50% more meeting time than Zoom's free plan and the same participant ceiling.

The practical advantage is logistics. If your team uses Gmail and Google Calendar, a Meet link attaches to every event automatically, and anyone can join from a browser tab with no install. Live captions are free, which is genuinely useful for noisy home offices and for anyone who needs them for accessibility.

**The real free-tier limits:** 
The 60-minute cap applies to group calls with three or more participants; one-on-one calls can run up to 24 hours.
 
You get a warning at the 50-minute mark, then the call ends at 60 minutes.
 The cutoff is a nuisance, but not fatal — 
the meeting link doesn't expire, so everyone can rejoin immediately and start a fresh 60 minutes
.

The bigger gap is recording. 
There's no built-in recording in the free plan
, and you also won't get breakout rooms or noise cancellation. 
Removing the 60-minute limit and adding intelligent noise cancellation comes with the first paid Workspace tier, while recording saved to Google Drive, breakout rooms, and polls arrive on the higher Business Plus plan.
 
On annual billing, those Workspace plans run $7, $14, and $22 per user per month for Business Starter, Standard, and Business Plus.


**Best for:** teams already using Google accounts who want the fewest possible steps between "let's hop on a call" and actually being on the call.

## Jitsi Meet — Best for Unlimited Meeting Length

Jitsi Meet is the pick if the time limit is the thing driving you away from Zoom. 
It's a fully open-source, encrypted video conferencing tool you can use all day, every day, for free — with no account needed.
 You go to meet.jit.si, name a room, and send the link. That's the whole onboarding process.

No sign-up is a bigger deal than it sounds for client calls and contractor check-ins. The person on the other end doesn't need a Google account, a Microsoft account, or a download — just a browser. 
Screen sharing is included, new members join via a generated link, and there are mobile apps alongside the web interface.


**The real free-tier limits:** the constraint isn't a paywall, it's capacity on the shared public server. 
The free public-use instance at meet.jit.si supports up to 100 participants.
 In practice, video quality on the public server degrades well before that ceiling, so treat it as a small-meeting tool rather than an all-hands platform. You also don't get the polish of commercial tools — no deep calendar integration by default, a plainer interface, and no vendor support desk when something goes sideways.

The escape hatch is unusual for a free product: 
Jitsi is 100% open source and freely available to use and develop with
, so 
you can set up your own Jitsi Meet instance or embed a room into your own website
. That means a technical team can self-host and get exactly the capacity and privacy posture it wants, at the cost of running a server.

**Best for:** privacy-conscious teams, long working sessions, and calls with outside collaborators who shouldn't have to create an account.

## Microsoft Teams Free — Best If You Want Chat and Calls Together

Teams Free is less of a Zoom replacement and more of a Slack-plus-Zoom replacement. You get persistent team chat, file sharing, and video meetings in one app, which is a real argument if you're currently paying for two tools.

**The real free-tier limits:** 
Microsoft Teams Free caps group meetings at 60 minutes with up to 100 participants.
 
One-on-one calls have no time limit on any plan, including the free version.
 
The free plan also includes unlimited chat, basic collaboration, and 5 GB of storage.


One quirk worth knowing before you schedule anything: 
both the length cap and the participant ceiling are set by the meeting organizer's license, not by who joins — if a free-Teams user organizes, the meeting ends at 60 minutes no matter who's invited.
 So if one person on your team has a paid license, have them host the long calls.

What's missing free: cloud recording, business email on your own domain, and admin/security controls. 
Paid options start with standalone Teams Essentials at $4 per user per month, or Microsoft 365 Business Basic at $7 per user per month, which adds custom business email, web Office apps, and 1 TB of OneDrive storage per user.
 Teams Essentials at $4 is one of the cheaper ways to kill a 60-minute limit if that's all you need.

**Best for:** small businesses already in the Microsoft ecosystem, or teams that want one app instead of two subscriptions.

## FAQ

**Which free option gives the longest meetings?**
Jitsi Meet. 
It's free to use all day, every day, with no account required
, and there's no built-in cutoff. Google Meet and Teams both stop group calls at 60 minutes on their free plans.

**Can I record calls for free on any of these?**
Not natively on Google Meet or Teams Free — 
Google Meet has no built-in recording in the free plan
, and Teams Free doesn't include cloud recording either. If recording matters, budget for a paid tier or use a separate screen recorder, and tell participants they're being recorded.

**Is the 60-minute limit actually a problem?**
Less than you'd think for internal standups and one-on-ones. 
Because the meeting link doesn't expire, everyone can rejoin and restart the clock.
 It's a problem for client calls, interviews, and workshops, where getting dropped mid-sentence looks sloppy — that's the point where the $4–$7 per user tiers start earning their money.

**Do participants need an account to join?**
On Jitsi, no. On Google Meet and Teams, guests can typically join from a browser link, but the host needs an account, and some meeting settings can require guests to sign in before being admitted.
