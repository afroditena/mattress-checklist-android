# Todoist vs Notion: Which To-Do App Should You Use in 2026?

**Short answer:** If you want a fast, reliable place to capture and finish tasks, pick Todoist. If your tasks are tangled up with notes, docs, project trackers, and a team wiki, pick Notion. Todoist is the better *to-do app*; Notion is the better *workspace that can hold a to-do list* — and the price gap between them is wider than most people expect.

## Pricing: Todoist Is Roughly Half the Cost

*Screenshot of notion.com ([source](https://www.notion.com/pricing))*

Todoist keeps it simple. 
It offers three plans: Beginner (free), Pro, and Business.
 
Pro runs $5/month billed annually ($60/year) or $7/month billed monthly, and Business is $8/user/month annually ($96/user/year) or $10/user/month monthly.
 Worth knowing: 
Todoist raised the Pro price effective December 10, 2025
, so older articles quoting $4 are out of date. 
Long-time subscribers may sit on a "Pro Legacy" plan that preserves their pre-June-2022 rate
, though 
paid features released after December 10, 2025 aren't added to Pro Legacy.


Notion has four tiers — Free, Plus, Business, and Enterprise — and costs more per seat. 
Notion's own pricing note lists Plus at $10 per member/month billed annually and $12 billed monthly
, with 
Business shown at $20, which is the yearly rate (the Notion for Startups page describes it as $20 per member paid annually).
 There's also a metered layer on top: 
Custom Agents run on credits billed separately at $10 per 1,000 credits.


Run the math for a five-person team on annual billing and Todoist Business lands around $480/year versus roughly $600/year for Notion Plus — and about $1,200/year if that team needs Notion Business. That doesn't make Notion overpriced; it's doing far more than task tracking. But if tasks are all you need, you're paying a premium for capability you won't use.

## Ease of Use: Opinionated vs. Open-Ended

Todoist is opinionated, and that's the point. You type "email Dana about the Q3 deck Friday 9am p1," and it parses the date, time, and priority for you. 
Smart Quick Add, task reminders, and list and board views are available even on the free Beginner plan.
 There's essentially no setup phase — you install it and start dumping tasks.

Notion is the opposite: a blank canvas. 
You manage tasks and projects through Kanban boards, calendar, and list views, track related information on each item, change statuses, collaborate, and work with spreadsheet-style databases.
 That flexibility is genuinely powerful, but you're the architect. A Notion task system is something you *design* — properties, filtered views, relations between a tasks database and a projects database — and then maintain. Some people love that. Others spend a weekend building a gorgeous dashboard and never open it again.

A useful gut check: if you've ever abandoned a productivity system because tweaking it became the hobby, Todoist's guardrails are a feature, not a limitation.

## The Feature That Actually Separates Them

Todoist's edge is *capture speed and nudging*. Recurring tasks, natural-language scheduling, priority flags, and saved filters are the everyday machinery of getting things done, and Todoist has spent years polishing them. 
Recent additions include calendar layouts for planning tasks over time and deadlines for tracking key dates.


Notion's edge is *context*. A task in Notion isn't just a line of text — it's a page that can hold the meeting notes, the spec, the client brief, and a link to the parent project. For knowledge work where "the task" and "the document" are inseparable, that's a real advantage no standalone to-do app can match.

The trade-off shows up in daily friction. Adding a quick task in Notion takes more clicks and more thought about where it belongs, and mobile capture has never felt as instant as Todoist's. Conversely, Todoist can't hold your team wiki, and it won't replace a project database with rollups and relations.

## Collaboration and AI

For teams, Todoist's Business plan is deliberately narrow: 
a shared team workspace, up to 500 team projects, calendar layout for team projects, granular activity logs, shared templates, 1,000 team members and guests, team project folders, roles and permissions, centralized billing, and SOC 2 Type II certification.
 That's a solid shared task layer — not a full project management suite. No Gantt charts, no dependencies.

Notion scales differently. Higher tiers add serious admin muscle — 
SAML SSO, granular database permissions, private teamspaces, and 90-day page history
, with 
Enterprise layering on SCIM provisioning, audit logs, unlimited page history, and a dedicated customer success manager.


AI is where budgets diverge most. Notion restructured in 2025: 
the standalone AI add-on was eliminated on May 13, 2025 and full AI folded into the Business tier, so you can no longer bolt it onto a $10 Plus plan — you pay $20 per seat for Business.
 
Business is required for Notion Agent and AI Meeting Notes.
 Todoist's AI sits on its far cheaper paid plans, but it's narrower in scope — it helps you phrase, break down, and organize tasks rather than summarize documents or search a knowledge base.

## Who Should Use Which

- **Solo professionals and freelancers tracking personal work:** Todoist. Pro at $60/year is hard to beat, and the free tier is genuinely usable if you stay under five projects.
- **Students and casual list-makers:** Todoist Beginner, free. Notion's free tier also works well if you want notes and tasks together.
- **Small teams sharing a task list:** Todoist Business. It's about half the per-seat cost of Notion Business and requires no system-building.
- **Teams whose work lives in docs, wikis, and trackers:** Notion. Tasks-plus-context in one workspace is worth the extra spend, and it consolidates tools you're probably paying for separately.
- **Teams that want built-in AI over their own content:** Notion Business — just budget the $20/seat honestly, plus credits if you lean on agents.
- **People who've bounced off complex tools before:** Todoist, every time.
- **Honestly? Many people run both:** Notion as the knowledge base, Todoist as the daily action list. The combined cost is still modest, and each tool does what it's actually good at.

## FAQ

**Can Notion fully replace Todoist?**
Functionally, yes — you can build a task database with due dates, priorities, and recurring items. Practically, it depends on whether you'll accept slower capture and weaker reminders in exchange for keeping everything in one place.

**Is Todoist's free plan enough?**
For a lot of people, yes. 
Beginner is permanent and free, with five personal projects, Smart Quick Add, list and board layouts, three filter views, and basic collaboration.
 The project cap is the usual reason to upgrade.

**Which is cheaper for a 10-person team?**
Todoist, clearly. Business runs $96 per user per year on annual billing, while Notion Business is listed at $20 per member per month annually — and monthly billing costs more on both.

**Do I have to pay annually to get the advertised price?**
Effectively, yes. 
The headline "$5" and "$8" Todoist figures are annual-billed rates; month-to-month costs more.
 Notion works the same way, so confirm the toggle on each vendor's pricing page before you commit a budget line — both companies have changed prices recently.
