**My verdict:** Use the free tier of an AI resume tool to catch keyword gaps and formatting that machines can't read — that part is real and takes twenty minutes. Paying $29–$50 a month for unlimited scans only makes sense if you're sending double-digit applications monthly and you're already getting nothing back. I'm leading with that because the whole category is sold on an implied promise — more interviews — that none of these vendors actually publishes evidence for, and you deserve to know that before the card comes out.

## What's Actually Going On

![jobscan.co official page screenshot](https://afroditena.github.io/mattress-checklist-android/assets/img/ai-resume-tools-in-2026-free-tiers-yes-subscriptions-no/2.png)
*Screenshot of jobscan.co ([source](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/ats-resume/))*

An AI resume tool in 2026 usually does three jobs stacked on top of each other, and it helps to separate them.

1. **Parse your resume the way software does.** Upload a file, and the tool reports what a machine can and can't extract. Jobscan's scanner checks formatting and readability alongside keyword content and returns a report broken into scored categories with issue counts.Every scan returns a Match Report with a Match Rate and a breakdown of gaps, with five scored categories and ATS detection when you add the company or job URL.
2. **Compare you to one specific job posting.** Jobscan's scanner page says its match rate draws on hard skills, education level when the posting asks for an advanced degree, job title, soft skills and other keywords.
3. **Write or rewrite the words.** This is where the LLM layer lives — bullets, summaries, cover letters. Teal's AI drafts and rewrites resume sections using your experience and a target job posting.

The free tiers are more generous than people assume. Teal's free plan includes unlimited resumes and PDF downloads, job tracking, and limited AI and analysis features. Jobscan's free plan runs on a monthly scan allowance, which is enough to test one or two real applications before you decide anything.

Paid pricing is where it bites. Teal+ costs $13 every 7 days, $29 every 30 days, or $79 every 90 days — note that the headline weekly price is the one you see first, and weekly billing is very easy to forget about. For Jobscan, third-party trackers checking the live plan page through September and October 2026 consistently list Premium at $49.95 monthly or $89.95 quarterly; I couldn't load Jobscan's own pricing page to confirm those numbers myself, so treat them as reported rather than verified-at-source.

## Where It Breaks

![support.jobscan.co official page screenshot](https://afroditena.github.io/mattress-checklist-android/assets/img/ai-resume-tools-in-2026-free-tiers-yes-subscriptions-no/3.png)
*Screenshot of support.jobscan.co ([source](https://support.jobscan.co/hc/en-us/articles/41334833854099-What-match-rate-should-I-aim-for))*

- **The score is the vendor's, not the employer's.** Jobscan says it plainly on its own scanner page: the Match Rate is a Jobscan visualization tool, not a score assigned by the employer's ATS. No recruiter ever sees that number.
- **The target is a heuristic, not a finish line.** Jobscan's support documentation suggests aiming for a 75%+ match rate, and its blog frames 75% or higher as being a good match and likely to get an interview. That's first-party guidance about a first-party metric. It isn't an industry standard, and it isn't a prediction anyone has published outcome data for.
- **Chasing the number degrades the resume.** Past a point, the only way to raise keyword overlap is to paste in terms you can't actually back up in an interview. A human reading your bullets notices that immediately.
- **Nothing here submits anything.** These tools tailor a document. You still fill out the Workday form by hand, one field at a time.
- **Scores are per-posting and unstable.** Change the target role and the number changes, because the comparison document changed. That makes it a tailoring tool, not a verdict on your resume.
- **The AI writing layer sounds like the AI writing layer.** Generated bullets trend toward generic verbs and inflated scope unless you rewrite them.

## The honest answer on "more interviews"

![tealhq.com official page screenshot](https://afroditena.github.io/mattress-checklist-android/assets/img/ai-resume-tools-in-2026-free-tiers-yes-subscriptions-no/4.png)
*Screenshot of tealhq.com ([source](https://www.tealhq.com/post/best-ai-resume-builders))*

I went looking for a vendor-published study showing that using one of these tools raises interview rates, with a sample size and a method. I couldn't find one. What I found was guidance about what score to aim for, which is a different claim entirely.

So here's how I'd reason about it instead. If your resume is a two-column PDF with the job titles in a text box, software may genuinely fail to read it, and fixing that converts zero reads into some reads. That's a real, mechanical gain. If your resume already parses cleanly and you're still getting silence, the bottleneck is probably fit, volume, or timing — and no keyword tool touches those.

I think that's the line the marketing deliberately blurs. The tools fix a parsing and vocabulary problem, then let you infer they fixed a hiring problem.

## What I Learned While Writing This (and What I Think)

**The vendors are more careful than their users.** I expected to find overreaching claims and instead found Jobscan disclaiming its own metric right on the product page. The "AI knows your ATS score" idea mostly comes from job-search forums and LinkedIn advice posts, not from the companies. That reframed the category for me.

**Weekly billing is the real pricing story.** Teal+ at $13 every 7 days reads as cheap and runs about twice the monthly rate if you leave it on through a three-month search. My take: if you subscribe to anything here, pick the longest billing period you'll genuinely use and put the cancellation date in your calendar the same day.

**Free tiers now cover the useful 80%.** Unlimited resumes, PDF downloads and job tracking on Teal's free plan plus a handful of free scans elsewhere gets most people through a normal search.

My concrete recommendation: run one free scan against the single job you most want, fix only the gaps where you have real evidence, keep the file to one column and standard headings, and stop. Pay for a subscription only if you're applying to ten-plus corporate roles a month and the cost per application drops below a couple of dollars. Some people will disagree — if you're career-switching and genuinely don't know the vocabulary of your target field, the keyword report is worth more than I'm giving it credit for, because it's teaching you the language, not just gaming a filter.

**Limits of this piece:** I read product and support documentation and compared pricing reports; I did not run my own resume through these tools or track callback rates. Any claim here about interview outcomes is my reasoning, not measured data.
